Discourse processing

Coreference and Discourse

Rank possible antecedents, expose competing cues, and test how a pronoun changes the interpretation of a discourse.

1 Read a discourse2 Inspect evidence3 Change salience4 Test interpretations
Learning objectives
  • Identify plausible antecedents for a referring expression.
  • Compare recency, prominence, compatibility, and repetition.
  • Distinguish a ranking from a resolved ground truth.
  • Use later discourse to assess coherence.
Step 1

Find what the pronoun could refer to

A referring expression can retrieve a person, object, or discourse entity introduced earlier.

Step 2

Inspect four sources of evidence

Values range from zero to one. They summarize a teaching analysis rather than universal linguistic facts.

Compatibility combines form and simplified world knowledge; prior mentions approximate discourse persistence.

Step 3

Change the salience model

Step 4

Follow an interpretation forward

Later sentences can reinforce, revise, or leave an antecedent choice unresolved.

ParaphrasePossible continuation

Why not always choose the nearest noun?

Recency is useful, but number, animacy, grammatical role, repeated mention, world knowledge, and later discourse can favor a different candidate.

Exit question

Which example remains genuinely ambiguous?

Use at least two competing cues and one coherent continuation to justify your answer.
