Linguistic similarity

How Far Apart Are Two Forms?

Transform one sequence into another, inspect the cheapest path, and question what the resulting distance measures.

1 Choose units 2 Fill the matrix 3 Change costs 4 Interpret distance
Learning objectives
  • Compute edit distance with dynamic programming.
  • Trace an optimal alignment.
  • Explain how units and operation costs define a measure.
  • Distinguish similarity in form from similarity in meaning.

Step 2

Each cell stores the cheapest cost of transforming one prefix into another. Select any revealed interior cell to revisit its calculation.

Step 3

Change the costs

A distance is not neutral: it formalizes which differences matter and by how much.

Suggested experiment

Raise substitution cost above 2.00. Does the optimal path still substitute different characters, or does it prefer a deletion followed by an insertion?

Step 4

Can edit distance measure meaning?

Not by itself. Cat and bat differ by one character, but that does not make their meanings more similar than words with larger spelling distances.

General lesson: before interpreting a distance, identify its units, allowable operations, costs, and the linguistic property it is supposed to represent.

Exit question

Which modeling choice changed your result most: the units or the operation costs?

Describe one comparison from the lab and state what the distance does—and does not—justify.
