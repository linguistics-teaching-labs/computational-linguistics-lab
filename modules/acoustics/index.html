Acoustic phonetics

Speech Sounds Made Visible

Generate an inspectable vowel-like signal and connect what you hear to time, frequency, pitch, and resonance.

1 Shape a vowel 2 Read a waveform 3 Find energy 4 Interpret formants
Learning objectives
  • Distinguish waveform, spectrum, and spectrogram.
  • Relate fundamental frequency to periodicity and perceived pitch.
  • Use F1 and F2 as acoustic cues to vowel quality.
  • Separate an illustrative synthesis from measured speech.
Step 1

Shape a vowel-like signal

A periodic source supplies harmonics; resonances strengthen energy near the formants.

Audio is generated locally after you press Play; the activity does not use a microphone or upload sound.

Step 2

Read change through time

The waveform shows air-pressure variation. Closely spaced cycles correspond to a higher F0.

Waveform

Move the F0 control and compare the spacing of repeating cycles.

Does a taller waveform mean a higher pitch?

No. Height represents amplitude. Pitch is more closely related to how often the pattern repeats each second.

Step 3

Find energy in frequency

A spectrum summarizes one interval; a spectrogram repeats that analysis across time.

Dashed lines mark the profile's three formant targets.

Spectrogram

Step 4

Interpret formants cautiously

F1 and F2 provide useful cues to vowel quality, but speakers and contexts vary.
