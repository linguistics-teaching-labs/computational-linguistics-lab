Why Can a Sentence Have Two Structures?

Build larger constituents from smaller spans, then compare the structures licensed by one grammar.

Learning objectives
  • Identify constituents licensed by a grammar.
  • Explain how a chart reuses smaller analyses.
  • Distinguish structural from lexical ambiguity.
  • Interpret probabilistic ranking as a preference, not a new parse.

Step 4

Interpret the ambiguity

A probability changes the ranking of licensed structures; it does not remove the ambiguity from the sentence.

Grammar

Determines which structures are possible.

Parser

Finds the structures efficiently.

Weights

Rank the structures using learned or assumed preferences.

Does the chart contain meanings?

No. It records categories that can cover each span. Humans use lexical meaning, discourse, and world knowledge to favor an interpretation.

Try it: compare the telescope and park examples. The same structural ambiguity can support different real-world interpretations.
